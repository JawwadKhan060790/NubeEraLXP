At NubeEra, learning begins with understanding how things 
work.
Young learners are naturally curious. They observe, ask 
questions, and explore the world around them. This book 
guides that curiosity by helping students recognise simple 
systems in everyday life.
Students learn that every system follows a pattern:
Input → Process → Output
Through real-life examples and guided activities, they 
understand how actions start, how changes happen, and 
how results are produced.
Learning follows a simple path:
Observe → Understand → Try → Improve

Digital Modelling — exploring ideas safely before building

2.1 What is an LED
Definition
An LED is a small electronic light. 
It glows when electricity flows through it. 
LEDs are used in toys, indicators, and electronic devices.
They are commonly used in simple circuits.
Example
An LED lights up when connected to a battery.
2.2 What is a switch
Definition
A switch controls the flow of electricity 
in a circuit. 
 It can turn the circuit on or off. 
 When the switch is closed, electricity flows. 
 When the switch is open, electricity stops.
Example
Pressing the switch turns the light on.
Summary
 »An LED produces light in a circuit.
 »A switch controls the light.
Did You Know?
LED lights use less electricity and last longer than 
many other types of bulbs.

3. Understanding basic circuit flow
Real-life example
A torch lights up because electricity flows from 
the battery to the bulb. 
If the connection breaks, the light stops.
Electricity always needs a path to travel.
3.1 What is a circuit
Definition
A circuit is a path through which electricity flows. 
The path connects the battery and electronic parts. 
Electricity moves through this path to power 
devices. 
If the path breaks, the circuit stops working.
Example
A battery connected to an LED creates a working 
circuit.
Summary
 »Electricity flows through a circuit.
 »A complete circuit allows devices to 
work.
Did You Know?
Every electronic device in your home works 
because of circuits inside it.
